Become a mentor

Be the mentor you wish you had in first year.

Been through first year? Help a first-year guy who is exactly where you were, and make a friend in the process. Informal, one to one, on your terms.

Voluntary. Confidential. Free for everyone.

What you get

Real training

Mentors are trained to listen and to refer, so you know what to say and when to point to more help.

Real experience

Leadership and volunteering you can add to your CV, with a program you helped start.

Real impact

A first-year student who talks to someone is one step closer to getting the help he needs.

Mentoring goes both ways. You help him, and you gain a real friend who will have your back too.

2nd or 3rd-year male students

Students who have been through first year and remember how it felt.

How it works

1. Match

Tell us a bit about you. We pair you with a trained mentor from your university.

Tap for an example
Example (fictional)

Maverick, 19, is stressed about his first midterms. He signs up and is paired with Samuel, a 3rd-year student in a similar program.

2. Meet

Chat informally, one to one, about school, stress or anything on your mind.

Tap for an example
Example (fictional)

Maverick and Samuel grab a coffee on campus. Samuel tells him how he handled his own first exams. No forms, just a chat.

3. Refer

If you need more, your mentor points you to your university's psychological services.

Tap for an example
Example (fictional)

After a few chats, Maverick says he can't sleep before exams. Samuel tells him how to reach the university's psychological services.

Why a mentor?

A first conversation (fictional example)

MaverickHey! I have an exam with Professor Lyna next week. Did you have her as a teacher?
Samuel, 3rd yearYes! I can tell you how it usually goes with her. And honestly, I was stressed like that last year too.
MaverickReally? I thought it would be awkward to ask for help.
Samuel, 3rd yearNot awkward. Just two students talking. Want to grab a coffee Thursday?
MaverickOk. Thursday works.

Why?

Someone who gets it

Questions

Is it confidential?

Yes. The conversations are informal and confidential.

Does it cost anything?

No. It is free for students.

Is a mentor a therapist?

No. Mentors listen, and refer you to your university's psychological services if you need more.

Who are the mentors?

2nd or 3rd-year male students who have been through first year, and who are trained to listen and to refer.
